Here's a problem almost every business using AI has, and almost nobody has named: **your AI assistant has no memory of your business, so every session starts from zero.**

Monday morning you spend twenty minutes explaining your pricing structure, your service area, and how you talk to customers. ChatGPT writes a genuinely excellent quote. Thursday you open a fresh session and it knows none of it — so you either re-explain (badly, because you're busy) or accept generic output. Your employee opens their own session and knows even less of what to explain, so the assistant invents details that sound plausible and aren't true.

The result: the quality of "AI at your business" swings wildly depending on who's using what AI and what's the user's state of mind.

![Business AI with and without a context pack](/spotlightskills/context-pack-diagram.svg)

## This is not a model problem

Don't blame the tool. Don't try a different model. Maybe pay for the premium model? Waiting for the next release is not an option. It won't fix anything because the models are already good enough. Like someone wanting to breakup says , "it's not you, it's me,"- **The missing ingredient isn't the model's intelligence, it's the context.**

Think about what you'd do with a new hire. You wouldn't hand them the phones on day one and hope. You'd tell them your prices, the jobs you don't take, how to talk to an angry customer, and the three things they must never promise. Then they'd be useful — not because they got smarter, but because they got briefed.

An AI assistant is the most capable new hire you'll ever have, and most businesses re-hire it from scratch every single morning without a briefing.

## What "consistency" is actually worth

The cost of this shows up in ways that don't look like an AI problem on the invoice:

- **Wrong details reaching customers.** An invented price, an outdated warranty term, a service area that includes a town you stopped covering. These are expensive and they erode trust quietly.
- **Time re-explaining.** Twenty minutes a session, several sessions a week, across everyone who uses the tool. That's real hours spent typing things you've typed before.
- **Output you have to rewrite.** Generic copy that sounds like every competitor because nothing in the prompt told the model what makes you different.
- **Staff who quietly stop using it.** The most common failure. People try AI, get inconsistent results, decide it "doesn't really work for our business," and go back to doing it manually — while a competitor with the same tools pulls ahead.

## The fix is a file, not a subscription

The durable solution is to write your business down once, properly, in a format the AI loads automatically at the start of every session.

That format now exists as an open standard. **Agent Skills** — a specification Anthropic published in December 2025 — defines a folder with a `SKILL.md` file: plain Markdown with a few lines of metadata. Adoption was unusually fast; roughly **40 products** support it, including Claude, ChatGPT and Codex, GitHub Copilot, Cursor, Gemini CLI, and VS Code. One file, portable across the tools your team actually uses, with no vendor lock-in.

What goes in it is unglamorous and decisive: canonical facts and real prices, what you sell and don't, your voice taught through real examples, and the rules that cost money when a machine guesses. [We've written the full anatomy separately](/blog/what-goes-in-a-business-ai-context-pack).

## We run our own business on one

Spotlight Links operates on a context pack. It carries our entity facts, our editorial rules, our conventions, and our [published operating numbers](/blog/how-long-does-100-dollars-claude-credits-last).

We spend 15–20 hours a week working alongside frontier models — that's not a side activity here, it's how the business runs. The day we wrote that file, zero of those minutes went back into re-explaining who we are, what we've already published, or how we write. Sessions start fluent. It is the highest-leverage hour of writing we've done.

## The honest limits

Two things this doesn't do, stated plainly because the confusion is being actively sold:

**It will not get you cited by AI search engines.** A context pack improves the AI _you hire_. Being recommended when a customer asks ChatGPT "who should I call?" is [entirely different work](/blog/how-to-get-recommended-by-chatgpt-gemini-perplexity-claude) — indexing, answer-shaped content, entity signals. And [putting a SKILL.md on your website does nothing at all](/blog/should-your-business-have-a-skill-md), because no crawler reads it there.

**It won't fix a vague business.** If your prices and boundaries aren't actually decided, no file can encode them. Writing one often surfaces that — which is uncomfortable and useful.

## Where to start

If your team touches AI at all, write down the ten things you find yourself re-explaining. That list _is_ the first draft. Most of the value arrives in the first hour of honest writing, before any format or tooling is involved.
