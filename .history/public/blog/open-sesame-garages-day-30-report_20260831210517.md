**Thirty days into the Open Sesame Garages program: 12 clicks, 119 impressions, 10.1% click-through rate, average position 11.4.** Small numbers. Published anyway, because the _shape_ of this data says far more than the volume does — and because nobody in this industry shows you month one.

Context first, since it decides how to read everything below. Open Sesame Garages is a garage door company in Oklahoma City, **less than a year old**, run by Abdul, its owner and only technician. The website has been live and working for **about one month**. It is **not on Google Maps yet**. It has **not been posted to a single social account**. No paid ads. No backlink campaign.

So this isn't a report on a finished program. It's a report on seeds.

![Open Sesame Garages day-30 search report](/blog/open-sesame/day-30-report.svg)

## What's working

### Position 1 on the two keywords that matter

`garage door repair in oklahoma city` — **average position 1.00**, one impression, one click, 100% CTR.
`garage door service okc` — **average position 1.00**.

Those are the commercial money queries for this business, and the site holds the top spot on both after roughly a month, with zero backlink work. That's the structural bet paying off: [answer-shaped content](/blog/how-to-get-recommended-by-chatgpt-gemini-perplexity-claude) with real prices and real service areas, clean technical foundations, proper schema, indexed properly from day one.

The impression counts are tiny because search volume for exact-match phrasings is tiny — but ranking #1 is the hard part. Volume follows surface area, and surface area is a content problem we know how to solve.

### A 10.1% CTR at average position 11.4

This is the number I'd point at if I could only show one. Typical organic CTR at position 11 runs somewhere around 1–2%. **Open Sesame is pulling 10.1% — roughly six times that.**

Translation: when this site appears in a result list, people choose it over things ranked _above_ it. That's titles, meta descriptions, and answer-shaped snippets doing exactly their job. You cannot buy that; you write it.

### 81% engagement rate

Of everyone who lands, ~81% engage rather than bounce. The `/blog` and `/faq` sections both show **100% engagement**. People arriving from search are finding what they came for and reading further. The content isn't decorative.

### The geography is precise

Of 25 attributable users: **Oklahoma City 11, Dallas 6, Fort Worth 1** — roughly **72% in or adjacent to the target market**, plus New York (that's us) and a handful of international noise. For a local service business, traffic composition matters more than traffic volume. Wrong-city visitors are a cost, not a win.

### Brand awareness is starting

`open sesame garage door` pulled **9 impressions** — the single most-searched query in the set. A month ago that number was zero. People are beginning to search for this business by name.

Which brings us to the problem.

## What's not working

### The branded query got 9 impressions and 0 clicks

This is the loudest signal in the entire dataset, and it is not subtle: **people search the business name, see the results page, and don't click.**

The cause is almost certainly what we haven't done yet. There is **no Google Business Profile**. There are **several other companies called "Open Sesame"** in garage doors across the country — Jacksonville, Las Vegas, and others. So a customer searching the brand gets a results page with no map pack, no photos, no reviews, no hours, no "call" button, and several same-named competitors. Nothing anchors the _right_ business.

This is the highest-value unfixed thing on the list, and it's not a content problem. It's an **entity** problem — the [GEO layer](/blog/how-ai-answer-engines-choose-citations): consistent name, address, phone, verifiable existence, real reviews, a map presence. That work is next, and it's the reason the honest answer to "when do we see results?" is 45–60 days, not 30.

### Service-area pages rank at positions 54–70

`door repair yukon ok` (54), `door repair mustang ok` (62), `door installation yukon ok` (68), `garage door repair yukon` (70). The pages exist and are indexed — they're just not competitive. Location pages without local entity signals behind them are, essentially, claims without evidence. They'll move once the Business Profile and citations exist, and once each page carries genuinely local specifics rather than a swapped city name.

### /contact: 8 impressions, 0 clicks

People see the contact page in results and don't click it. Either the title and description aren't giving a reason to, or — more likely — someone searching "contact information" wants the phone number _in the result_, not a page to visit. That's a structured-data and snippet fix.

### The homepage is carrying everything

**All 12 clicks went to the homepage**, which also took ~70% of impressions. Meanwhile the FAQ pages are ranking respectably — `/faq/garage-door-spring-replacement-cost-okc` at position 8.6, `/faq/diy-garage-door-spring-repair-safety` at 5.0 — but converting no clicks yet.

A healthy local site spreads entry points across many pages, so it can be found for many different questions. Right now there's one front door. The deep pages are ranked but under-impressed, which is a volume problem, not a quality problem.

### 119 impressions is a small surface

The site ranks well for the few things it appears for. It just doesn't appear for enough things yet. More questions answered properly = more surfaces = more impressions. This is the most mechanical, most solvable item on the list.

## The honest timeline

Here's the part most agencies won't put in writing.

**Days 0–30 is not a results phase. It's the planting phase.** Content gets built, structured, and published; the technical foundation goes in; pages get indexed. You're putting seeds and crumbs on the ground to find out which birds show up — and some of what shows up will be worms, and some will be feathered things from other planets entirely. You don't get to know in advance.

**Days 45–60 is when the first real layer of signal arrives.** Entity work starts compounding, non-branded volume climbs, and the pages that were merely indexed start ranking.

**Three to six months** is when authority compounds and the numbers stop being small.

We are at day 30. Everything above is a _leading indicator_, and the leading indicators are unusually good: #1 rankings on the core commercial queries, CTR at six times the positional norm, 81% engagement, right cities. The lagging indicators — volume, branded clicks, service-area rankings — are exactly what the next 30 days are for.

Anyone who shows you a triumphant month-one report is either showing you a business that already had authority, or showing you a chart with no y-axis.

## What we do next for Abdul

In priority order, because the data ordered it for us:

1. **Google Business Profile, verified.** Fixes the branded-search hole, unlocks the local map pack, and puts a phone number and hours directly in results. Highest leverage by a wide margin.
2. **Citations and consistency.** Same name, address, phone everywhere — the entity signals that also make [AI engines confident enough to cite a business](/faq/how-to-get-your-business-cited-by-chatgpt).
3. **Real reviews, surfaced.** Abdul has genuinely happy customers. Right now they're not visible where it counts.
4. **Deepen service-area pages.** Local specifics per city, not a template with the name swapped.
5. **Expand the answer surface.** More questions, answered with real numbers — the material that's already ranking at positions 5–8.6 when we publish it.
6. **Social presence.** Not for the followers — for the entity signals and the branded SERP.
