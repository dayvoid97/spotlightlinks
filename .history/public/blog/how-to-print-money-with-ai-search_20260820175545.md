For twenty years the way to be the answer was to rank on Google — ten blue links, three ads on top, and a searcher who does all the qualifying work themselves. Then AI ate search. Or more precisely: search got AI, and the throne stopped belonging to one company.

Look at where American attention actually sits in mid-2026. Similarweb's May 2026 numbers put ChatGPT at 58.3% of US AI-assistant web visits, Gemini at 19.3%, Claude at 13.4%. ChatGPT alone reports around 900 million weekly users globally, with roughly 330 million prompts a day coming from the United States. Pew's 2026 work has 44% of US adults having used ChatGPT, up from 18% in 2023. Weekly chatbot use among US adults sits near half the adult population — call it 130 million people, every week, in this country alone.

I want to be careful here, because this is where marketers start lying. Google is not dead. Google still handles the overwhelming majority of all search queries on Earth. What has changed is that a large and fast-growing slice of the _high-intent, question-shaped_ queries — the ones where someone describes a problem and wants to be told what to do — now get answered by a model instead of a results page.

Those are the queries that were always worth money. Those are the ones moving.

## Why this traffic is worth more than Google traffic

A Google result is a guess. An AI answer is a recommendation.

When someone clicks through from ChatGPT, the engine has already heard their problem, compared their options, and named you as the one worth calling. The qualifying happened _before_ the click. They arrive further down the funnel than any Google visitor has ever arrived.

The measurements are all over the place in magnitude but they point the same direction every single time:

- **Semrush (2026)** — AI-driven visitors convert at 4.4× standard organic, across industries.
- **Adobe Analytics (April 2026)** — AI-referred shoppers to US retail sites converted 42% better than non-AI traffic in March, a record for the channel.
- **Microsoft Clarity**, across 1,200+ publisher sites — LLM-referred visitors signing up at 1.66% versus 0.15% from organic.
- **Ahrefs' own data** — 0.5% of traffic from AI search driving 12.1% of signups.

And the honest counterweight, which most agencies will not show you: a 12-month GA4 study of 94 ecommerce brands found ChatGPT referrals converting at 1.81% against 1.39% for non-branded organic. Real, but a 31% lift — not a 5×. For commodity ecommerce the premium is thin or absent. Nobody needs an AI recommendation for a phone case.

**The premium is enormous for considered purchases.** Contractors, home services, medical and optical practices, specialty retail, professional services, B2B. Anything where a person needs information before they can choose. If that's you, the number is real and it is sitting there unclaimed.

## Is this a secret?

No. And anyone selling it to you as a secret is selling you something else.

Everything I'm describing is published. The mechanics of how models retrieve and cite are documented. The schema vocabularies are open standards. The crawler user-agents are public. I have written most of it down on this site and given it away.

So why does it work?

Because **almost nobody is doing it.** The gap between "publicly known" and "actually implemented" is where every fortune in this business has ever been made. Ninety-nine percent of real-world businesses have a website built to look nice to a human and to be indexed by a 2015 search engine. It is structurally invisible to an answer engine. Not penalized — invisible. There is nothing there for a model to lift.

That's not a secret. That's a window. Windows close.

And here's the part I find genuinely good about it: this window is fair. AEO and GEO don't have a media budget gate. A garage door company in Oklahoma City can out-cite a national franchise because citations are earned by being the most specific, most structured, most obviously-true source on a question — not by outbidding anyone. Content is the cost of entry, and content is available to everybody.

So pick your business. Add the content. And know upfront that you're going to need _a lot_ of it, and that it has to be new, unique, and genuinely useful to the engine. Rewriting what's already indexed gets you nothing. The model already has that.

## The actual playbook

Here's the sequence, in order, with the deeper reading on each step.

**Step 1 — Let the crawlers in.** Most sites are accidentally blocking the exact bots they need. GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended — each does a different job, and blocking the wrong one makes you invisible while blocking the right one protects you. → [Which AI crawlers should I allow?](/faq/which-ai-crawlers-should-i-allow)
