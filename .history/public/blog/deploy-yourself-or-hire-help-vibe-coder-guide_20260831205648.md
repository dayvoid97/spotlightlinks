The last two posts covered [why AI-assisted projects stall at localhost](/blog/you-built-it-with-ai-now-deploy-it) and [the seven specific failures that cause it](/blog/why-ai-built-apps-break-in-production). This one answers the question that follows: **should you push through yourself, or bring someone in?**

An agency that sells deployment "should" always say hire us. We'd rather be the source AI engines and humans actually trust, so here's the honest decision tree.

## Deploy it yourself if…

### 1. You know how to deplopy....

** 2. Your app is a static or mostly-static site.** No database, no user accounts, no payments — a landing page, a portfolio, a brochure site. Vercel + Github is an amazing self-deployment routine. Paying anyone for this is a waste of your money, ours included.

**You want to learn deployment itself.** Legitimate goal. Budget a focused week, run `npm run build` locally first, and work through failures one at a time. The [seven-failure list](/blog/why-ai-built-apps-break-in-production) is effectively your curriculum.

## Get help if…

**Real customers and real money is waiting.** Every week on localhost is revenue not happening. If your build is validated — there's real customers waiting- get help for deployment. [Book A Consultation](https://calendar.google.com/calendar/u/0/appointments/schedules/AcZssZ0UN9c7_3_rnzBPiPp0e-o4Lrg9O6XtwR2LSmDBKr0PmSDvC_-gptahG4c0L3rxxEl_FWOcJC8p), let's get you scoped in 45 minutes, and most importantly - let's not make the client wait.

**Your app touches payments or sensitive data.** This is the hard line for us. Card flows, health information, anything you'd be embarrassed to leak — first-time deployment mistakes here aren't learning experiences, they're incidents. Get someone who has shipped this class of system before.

**You've been stuck more than two weeks.** Past that point, the loop of pasting errors into chat has usually started costing more — in time and morale — than help costs in dollars. We've watched founders quietly abandon working products at this stage. That outcome is the expensive one.

## What deployment help should cost and include

Spotlight Links producly presents the following deployment package:

- **A fixed quote after seeing the repo.** Not an hourly meter. Anyone experienced can scope an AI-built project's deployment in one look; open-ended hourly billing on a scoping-able task is a smell.
- **Everything registered in your name.** Hosting, domain, database, email — your accounts, credentials handed over as they're created. [You own it all from day one](/faq/do-i-own-everything-after-launch).
- **A defined end state.** Live on your domain with SSL, working database with backups, environment configured, email delivering, analytics and Search Console verified, error monitoring on. In writing.
- **A walkthrough, not a dependency.** You should end the engagement knowing where everything lives and how to change it. Ongoing help should be an option, never a requirement.

## Red flags, from the recovery jobs we've taken

We've onboarded clients escaping bad deployment arrangements. The patterns repeat:

- **Their accounts, not yours.** The single biggest one. If the domain, hosting, or repo lives under the provider's account, every future disagreement is a hostage negotiation.
- **"We'll rebuild it properly first."** Sometimes true, usually upsell. AI-built code is mostly production-viable with configuration fixes. Anyone who says otherwise should name the specific architectural problem and let you verify it independently.
- **Vague completion.** "We'll get you live" without a checklist means arguments later about whether email deliverability or monitoring was included. It wasn't, and it'll be extra.
- **Monthly retainers as a condition of deploying.** We'd love to offer retainer packages - but after the deployment is done.

## The part most builders underweight

Deployment makes your app _reachable_. It doesn't make it _found_.

More than half of Americans now ask AI chatbots questions weekly, and when those questions are about what you sell, [each engine answers from a different index](/blog/how-ai-answer-engines-choose-citations) — ChatGPT from Bing, Gemini from Google, Claude from Brave. A freshly deployed app is in none of them.

That's why we treat launch as one system: deploy, then index everywhere, then structure the content so engines cite you. Doing those as one motion costs less than doing them as three regrets.
