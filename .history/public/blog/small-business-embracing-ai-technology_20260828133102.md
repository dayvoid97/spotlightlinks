# The Local Businesses Embracing New Technology — What Sets Them Apart

After having 300+ IRL conversations with small business owners, a pattern has emerged for Spotlight Links. We think this is very interesting and anyone trying to do better in sales should take of this pattern. A specific type of business is leaning hard into AI, and they're not the ones you'd necessarily expect. Here's what we're seeing.

## 1. Business Owners Embracing AI Tend to Skew Younger

The businesses moving fastest aren't run by digital natives in the Silicon Valley sense — they're run by owners in their 30s and 40s who grew up alongside the internet and have watched enough platform shifts to recognize a new one when it arrives. They understand that AI is about to reshape how customers find local businesses, and they're not waiting around to find out how.

These owners already have a working knowledge of SEO and are tracking how it's evolving into GEO — generative engine optimization, the practice of getting cited and recommended inside AI answers rather than just ranking in blue links. More than half have started distinguishing between how ChatGPT and Gemini actually surface businesses differently, and a striking share — north of 60% — are already fluent in the broader AI ecosystem shaping their market. They're watching the Gemini rollout with Google closely, because they know it changes the game again.

They also tell us Spotlight Links' pricing feels fair for what it delivers — and a good number have said they're seriously considering signing on, though the August seasonality slowdown that hits a lot of small business spending has made timing tricky for some.

## 2. Recurring-Client Businesses Are All In

The second pattern is about business model, not age. We're talking therapy practices, massage studios, boxing gyms, vision and eye-care shops, insurance agencies, garage door repair companies — the kind of businesses where every single client relationship translates into meaningful, predictable revenue. Losing one client to a competitor that shows up first in an AI answer isn't a rounding error for these businesses. It's felt.

These are consistently the industries most willing to invest in a Spotlight Links visibility probe. They're genuinely curious where they currently stand in the competitive landscape, and once they see the results, a lot of them start thinking seriously about adjusting their content and marketing for AEO/GEO. At $79, the report feels like an easy yes to them.

**So what does the $79 probe actually do?** It gives a business an objective, outside-in look at exactly where they stand in AI search results right now — running real customer-style questions ("best massage therapist near me," "who does garage door repair in my area") through the AI engines people actually use, then showing whether your business gets mentioned, whether it gets cited as a source, and critically, whether _you're_ the one getting recommended or your competitor is. No guesswork, no vague promises — just a clear read on your current visibility and where the gaps are.

## So What's the Takeaway?

Ownership of American small businesses is quietly shifting — from Boomers to younger Gen X and millennial owners. We're seeing it firsthand. Whether that business gets absorbed by a franchise or national brand, gets reinvented from within by family, or gets sold to a younger, more internet-fluent owner, the direction is the same: a new generation is taking the wheel, and they're bringing a different relationship with technology along with them.

Spotlight Links helps these businesses stay relevant and hold a competitive edge — by giving them clear visibility into how AI sees them, and a concrete path to improving it.
